# How to Talk to Your Parents About Aged Care

If you've been putting off this conversation, you're not alone, and there's no single right way to have it. What tends to help most is starting small, starting early, and treating it as an ongoing chat rather than one big talk you need to get right the first time.

Here's how to think about timing, what to actually say, and what to do if your parent isn't ready yet.

## When to Start the Conversation

There's no perfect moment, but there are good ones. A change you've noticed, something a friend mentioned about their own parents, or just a relaxed moment together over a cup of tea, any of these can be a natural way in.

If your parent is still active and independent, that's not a reason to wait. It's actually one of the easiest times to talk, because nothing feels urgent yet. Starting early gives you more room to think things through together, rather than scrambling to make decisions later.

## How to Bring It Up

### Start Small

"Have you thought about aged care?" can feel like a lot to answer all at once. Try something more specific instead, like "What would you want to happen if the stairs became too much?" It's easier to answer, and it opens the door without asking your parent to plan their whole future in one sitting.

### Ask What Matters to Them

Your parent almost certainly has thoughts on where they'd want to live, who they'd want involved in decisions, and what a good day looks like for them. Leading with these questions, rather than jumping straight to logistics, helps the conversation feel like it's about them and their life, not a problem you're trying to solve.

### Give It Time

Most families don't sort everything out in one conversation, and that's completely normal. Think of this as something you'll come back to now and then, gently, rather than a single milestone to tick off.

## If Your Parent Isn't Ready to Talk

It's common for a parent to change the subject, brush off a concern, or say they'd rather not discuss it right now. That doesn't mean the conversation failed. It usually just means the moment wasn't quite right.

A few things that tend to help:

- Leave it for another day, rather than pushing when they're not open to it.

- Gently ask what's behind the hesitation. Often it's less about the practical details and more about wanting to hold onto independence.

- Bring in a sibling, a trusted friend, or an independent adviser if the conversation keeps stalling between just the two of you.

If you're ever genuinely worried about your parent's safety or wellbeing, their General Practitioner is a good next call. They can help guide what comes next.

## Understanding What Comes Next: The Aged Care Assessment

Once the conversation is underway, the next practical step is usually an aged care assessment through the Single Assessment System (SAS). This looks at your parents’ current needs and helps determine what kind of government funding they're eligible for, whether that's Commonwealth Home Support Program (CHSP), Support at Home (home care), or Residential Aged Care.

Respite care in an aged care home is worth mentioning too, and it can be a gentle way to ease into things. It gives your parent a chance to try extra support for a short period, say 1-3 weeks, without it being a permanent decision, which often makes the bigger conversation feel less final and more like a step you're taking together.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### When should I talk to my parents about aged care?

Sooner than it might feel necessary. Raising it while your parent is still active and independent gives you both more time to think things through, rather than deciding quickly during a change in circumstances.

### What if my parent refuses to discuss aged care?

Give it time, and come back to it another day rather than pushing in the moment. Gently understanding what's behind the reluctance, often tied to independence, can help you find a better way in next time.

### What is an ACAT assessment?

An ACAT, now generally referred to as an aged care assessment, looks at what type and level of aged care support a person is eligible for, including Home Care Packages and residential care.
